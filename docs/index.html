The problem

A transverse feedback linearizing controller is undefined on a singular set, such as zero speed for a unicycle or zero thrust for a quadrotor. Only some initial states converge to the path. Which ones?

The idea

Where the controller is defined, the transverse dynamics are exactly linear. The state-constrained Zubov value function then splits into a known quadratic cost-to-go plus a safety value function, and only the latter is learned.

The result

An estimate with an inner layer verified by interval branch and bound and an outer layer tested on samples. It covers on average – and – of the simulated region of attraction on the unicycle and – on the planar quadrotor.

The question

TFL guarantees are local. How large is the region of attraction?

Within each animation, every vehicle runs the same controller. While a vehicle stays admissible, its transverse error obeys linear, exponentially stable dynamics. Some vehicles reach the singular set or a state constraint first, and then the controller fails. The constrained region of attraction is the set of starting states that never reach the singular set or leave the state constraints.

The idea

Half of the value function is known in closed form.

The state-constrained Zubov value function of Grüne and Zidani is finite exactly on the constrained region of attraction. On the admissible set the error coordinates obey ż = Az, so the running cost integrates to a quadratic, and the value function splits in two:

The first term is the quadratic cost-to-go. It is exact, and it decreases strictly along every trajectory in the admissible set until the trajectory reaches the path. The second is a safety value function: the worst future margin to the constraints. It is the only part a network has to learn, and it is learned by fitted value iteration from short simulated steps, without infinite-horizon labels. In the traces above, the orange part always falls. The blue part is positive only while the vehicle will later approach a constraint, and it never grows along a run.

A run that passes close to a state constraint, the face x₂ = −2. The bar shows V at the current state, split into the known part and the part a network learns.

Two layers

Push the level. See where the estimate breaks.

The estimate is a sublevel set {v < c₂} of the learned function, joined to a sublevel set of the quadratic cost-to-go that interval branch and bound verifies. The level c₂ is set by counterexamples, states where v fails to decrease. Drag the level well past the selected value and watch red states appear: the estimate then contains states outside the simulated region of attraction.

What a million samples buy

The outer layer is tested, not proved. The test still says a lot.

The direct test of unsafe states draws N independent samples of the box and simulates each sample in the estimate until it reaches the verified inner layer. If k of them are unsafe, then with confidence 1 − 10⁻⁶ and up to the integration error, the unsafe states occupy at most ε of the box, where ε is the one-sided Clopper–Pearson bound.

What holds, and how

Three kinds of claims, kept apart.

proved

The region and the split

On the admissible set, the constrained region of attraction of a TFL controller is its largest forward invariant subset, and the value function splits into the quadratic cost-to-go and the safety value function.

verified

The inner layer

A sublevel set of the quadratic cost-to-go, checked by interval branch and bound in floating-point arithmetic with outward rounding. It does not depend on the network, and {v < c₁} lies inside it for every candidate.

tested on samples

The outer layer

The learned sublevel set, tested with 10⁶ samples. A scenario bound limits the missed counterexamples, and a direct test of unsafe states limits the states it wrongly includes.

Results

Twelve systems, five methods.

Coverage of the simulated region of attraction, mean over four seeds. Every method except sum-of-squares passes the same level selection and final test. Toggle methods, and hover a marker for its value. The physics-informed baselines win on most equilibrium benchmarks, and the plot shows where.

Sum-of-squares is not applied to the TFL closed loops, which are rational with trigonometric terms and singular, and was not run on the obstacle benchmarks. The quadrotor is the hardest case: counterexamples hold its level low, and the bound V ≥ γqex alone then excludes – of its simulated region of attraction.

Other closed loops

The same learning applies to closed loops with an exponentially stable equilibrium.

With the quadratic form of the linearization in place of the exact cost-to-go, the method applies to closed loops with an exponentially stable equilibrium. A sublevel set of that quadratic form, verified by interval branch and bound, is again the inner layer. On nine benchmarks it covers, after fine-tuning, the most on the 3-D system with a state constraint and an obstacle. Physics-informed baselines trained on simulated labels, with a scale searched on the reference simulation, cover more on the other –.
